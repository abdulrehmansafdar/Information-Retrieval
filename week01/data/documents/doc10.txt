Natural Language Processing

Natural language processing, usually shortened to NLP, allows a computer to work with human language. The software receives raw text or speech and converts it into a form that a model can analyse. The first step is tokenization, in which a long document is split into small units such as words or parts of words and each unit receives a number. Counting these numbers, called term frequency, gives a simple first description of a document. Modern NLP systems use a transformer, a neural network design in which every token can look at the other tokens at the same time. This design produced the large language models now used for translation, question answering and summarisation of text. Because the meaning of a word changes with context, accuracy remains a difficulty. NLP is also applied in education, in search engines and in software that checks grammar and spelling.
