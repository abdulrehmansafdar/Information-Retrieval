Title: Migraine
Specialty: Neurology
Code: G43
Updated: 2024

## Overview
Migraine is a common neurological condition that causes repeated attacks of moderate to severe headache. The attacks are usually on one side of the head and throb. They can last from a few hours to three days. Migraine runs in families and is more common in women. Triggers include stress, poor sleep, missed meals, bright light, hormonal changes and some foods.

## Symptoms
Many attacks are preceded by an aura: visual changes such as zigzag lines or blind spots, or tingling in the hand or face. The headache is often accompanied by nausea, vomiting and sensitivity to light and sound. Some people feel tired or low for a day or two afterwards.

## Diagnosis
Migraine is diagnosed from the history of attacks, because there is no single test. A neurological examination rules out other causes. Imaging is reserved for atypical or worsening headaches, for example a headache that starts suddenly or that changes in character.

## Treatment
Attacks are treated early with a triptan or a non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drug, and an anti-nausea medicine can help. People who have frequent attacks may take a daily preventive medicine, such as a beta blocker, an antiepileptic or a newer injectable drug. A headache diary helps find triggers, and regular sleep and meals reduce attacks.
