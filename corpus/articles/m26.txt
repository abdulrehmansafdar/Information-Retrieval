Title: Osteoarthritis
Specialty: Musculoskeletal
Code: M19
Updated: 2025

## Overview
Osteoarthritis is the most common form of arthritis. It is a wear-and-tear condition in which the cartilage that cushions the ends of bones gradually breaks down, and the bone beneath it changes. It most often affects the knees, hips, hands and spine. Age, previous joint injury, obesity and repeated strain on a joint all increase the risk. Osteoarthritis is not caused by inflammation of the whole body, unlike rheumatoid arthritis.

## Symptoms
The usual symptoms are joint pain that gets worse with activity and eases with rest, stiffness after sitting or in the morning that lasts a short time, and a grating feeling in the joint. Swelling and bony lumps can develop in the finger joints. The joint may become less steady as the disease progresses.

## Diagnosis
Diagnosis is usually clinical, based on the pattern of symptoms and an examination of the joint. An X-ray shows loss of joint space, bone spurs and changes in the bone. Blood tests are normal in osteoarthritis, and they are used to rule out inflammatory arthritis.

## Treatment
Exercise, especially strengthening and low-impact activity, is the most effective treatment. Weight loss reduces the load on the knees and hips. Paracetamol and anti-inflammatory creams or tablets relieve pain. Physiotherapy, supportive braces and walking aids help. Joint replacement surgery is an option when pain and disability are severe and other treatment has not worked.
