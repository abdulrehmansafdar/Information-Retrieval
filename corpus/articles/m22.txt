Title: Fatty Liver Disease
Specialty: Gastroenterology
Code: K76
Updated: 2024

## Overview
Fatty liver disease is the build-up of fat inside liver cells in people who drink little or no alcohol. It is now the most common chronic liver condition. The main driver is metabolic: obesity, type 2 diabetes, high blood pressure and high cholesterol. Over time, some cases progress to inflammation, scarring and, rarely, cirrhosis or liver cancer. Many people with it have no symptoms.

## Symptoms
Most people feel no symptoms. Some notice tiredness or a dull ache under the right ribs. Signs of advanced liver disease, such as jaundice, swelling of the legs or abdomen, and confusion, appear only when scarring is severe.

## Diagnosis
Liver blood tests often show raised enzymes, but normal results do not rule out disease. An ultrasound scan shows fat in the liver. Liver stiffness measured by elastography or a blood-based score estimates how much scarring is present, and this decides how closely the patient is followed.

## Treatment
The main treatment is weight loss of around 7 to 10 percent of body weight, through diet and regular activity. Good control of diabetes, blood pressure and cholesterol is part of the plan. Alcohol is reduced or avoided. Newer medicines are being studied, but no tablet has yet been approved specifically for fatty liver in every patient.
